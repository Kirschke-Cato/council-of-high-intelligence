## 1 · Executive Conclusion

**Yes — Cato can run a 60–90-day relaunch almost entirely on existing material, but only by counting repackaging and re-editing as the work.** Across all properties there are roughly **20 relaunch-caliber assets**: 2 finished festival documentaries, 1 four-part docuseries, 3–4 proven video essays, 2 Friedman Prize films, ~16 short explainer episodes needing re-editions, and one compilable micro-doc library. Direct "just upload it" material is scarce; "strong footage wearing the wrong packaging" is abundant.

**The strongest library is not where the brief assumed.** Ranked by relaunch value:

1. **The main Cato channel itself.** It already produced the new identity in Q4 2024 and then buried it: *Faces of Globalization* (4-part on-location docuseries, 1.8–2.9K views each — verified), *The Paper Tiger* (Norberg scripted essay, **11.5K views**, the channel's best recent produced performer — verified), and *Free Jimmy Lai* (10:06 archival-driven Friedman film with a live news peg — verified). The relaunch does not need to invent a voice; it needs to resume and properly package November 2024's.
2. **Sphere** — smallest library, biggest assets: **Fulfilling the Promise** (~50-min documentary, four classrooms and the Declaration of Independence, festival-screened, public release pending — **the anchor premiere, requires internal confirmation of date/windows**) and **Voices of Tomorrow** (14:32, four festival awards, published Dec 19 2025 on a micro-channel at 1.5K views — verified; its trailer, oddly, already sits on the main channel at 871 views).
3. **Libertarianism.org** — one A-grade documentary (**Rediscovering F. A. Hayek**, 21 min, Dec 2024) and a promising monthly explainer line (**Learning Hub**, 7 eps since Oct 2025) whose L.org branding is baked into the narration — B-bucket re-editions, not re-uploads.
4. **Human Progress** — **zero usable 2024+ videos** (recent output is 55-min Zoom podcasts; verified samples at 177–2.8K views). Its value is the 2021–22 **Heroes of Progress** micro-doc library (~35–40 eligible eps, verified 7.5K–29.2K views each) as *compilation raw material*, plus a rights trap: the best "Human Progress" content (Norberg's *New and Improved*) belongs to the **Free To Choose Network**, not Cato.

**The duplication fear is mostly backwards.** True cross-channel duplication found: essentially one item (the Voices trailer/film split). The real disease is **fragmentation** — award-winning, Cato-funded films scattered across micro-channels and Brightcove pages where no audience will ever find them.

**Biggest gaps needing eventual new production** (§7): a recurring host-led show, news-reactive quick-takes, domestic-policy explainers (housing/healthcare/immigration/criminal justice), and a Shorts-native pipeline. The library funds roughly 12 weeks; production planning must restart by week 8.

## 5 · Events Recommendation

**Do not launch a new public "Cato Events" YouTube channel.** An events channel with no discovery intent is a public hard drive with a brand attached. The evidence this study produced: raw events on the main channel earn 300–2,500 views while actively defining the channel's identity downward, and "catovideo1" — the abandoned-channel candidate — currently resolves to an unrelated third-party channel (**requires internal confirmation**; no recoverable Cato assets found there).

**Recommended operating model:**
- **Main channel:** documentaries, explainers, films, human stories, select major-name interviews, and tightly packaged excerpts. Nothing else leads the feed.
- **Full events:** primary home is **cato.org** (Brightcove already hosts everything). Where a YouTube copy is institutionally necessary (embeds, sharing), upload **unlisted** and link from the event page. Unlisted uploads serve every archival function without polluting the channel's public identity.
- **Legacy archive:** leave existing public event uploads in place — reorganize playlists/homepage so new visitors never see them, but do not mass-delete or mass-unlist (destroys accumulated equity and breaks inbound links; the channel's deletion habit is already hurting it).
- **Exceptional guests (the Musk/Milei rule):** always two assets — a packaged, cold-opened, retitled interview for the main channel (the Reason Cleese/Taibbi model, 2.4M/1.6M views on event footage) and the full recording archived on cato.org/unlisted.

## 6 · Duplicate-Content Strategy

Full policy research with official citations lives in the appendix of the research file; the operational summary:

**What's actually true (from official YouTube documentation):**
- There is **no documented "duplicate penalty"** — but ranking signals (per-query watch time, comments, links) attach to a single video ID, so two copies **split** equity and neither inherits the other's history. The Copyright Match Tool only protects the *first* uploader.
- **Same-org ownership grants no exemption** from the reused-content policy: "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator." The bar is a *meaningful difference* a viewer can see.
- **Re-export tricks are worse than useless:** cropping, filters, and re-encoding are named examples of "detection evasion" in YouTube's spam policy. Content ID matching is perceptual, not file-based — bitrate/codec/logo/first-seconds changes defeat nothing and convert an innocent question into documented bad faith. A new title + thumbnail on the *same file* on a *different* channel is not a new product. (On your **own** channel, retitling your own upload is fine and is the core Week-0 move.)
- **Content ID internal claims are a non-issue for Cato** — Content ID requires exclusive rights to content "frequently uploaded" by others; Cato almost certainly doesn't qualify (`[ASSUMPTION]`), so no Cato channel can auto-claim another. The formal allowlist machinery doesn't apply; coordination is contractual/internal. Third-party music claims are the real claim risk on re-edits.
- **The purpose-built solution exists: YouTube Collabs** (launched Sept 2025) — up to 5 collaborator channels on one upload, recommended to *all* collaborators' audiences, revenue to the posting channel. This is the designed answer to "one video, several affiliated audiences."

**Decision tree:**
1. **Original still performing** (ranks for its query, has links/comments)? → leave it; playlist + channel-section + community-post it on main; queue a future *re-edited* Cato edition if the story deserves one.
2. **New release Cato controls the timing of** (Fulfilling the Promise, future Learning Hub eps)? → publish **once**, on the channel that fits the audience — for relaunch purposes, that's main — and **Collab-tag** the affiliated channels.
3. **Back-catalog piece worth real editorial work?** → substantive Cato edition (new cold open / narration / restructure / added graphics — the §"meaningful difference" bar), original stays up, provenance in the description.
4. **Verbatim republish genuinely needed** (source channel winding down / video never found an audience)? → migrate once: upload to main with provenance, **unlist (don't delete)** the original.
5. **Everything else** → clips, Shorts, and adaptations pointing at the original.

**Applied to the three properties:** Heroes of Progress → branch 5 + compilations (branch 3). Learning Hub back-eps → branch 3 (brand line is in the narration). Future Learning Hub → branch 2 (Collabs). Voices of Tomorrow → branch 3 or 4 (micro-channel, 1.5K views). Fulfilling the Promise → branch 2, premiere on main. Norberg *New and Improved* → none: **not Cato's property**; license or cross-promote only.

**Legal/licensing checks before any republish (all require internal confirmation):** music licensing scope per video (festival vs. YouTube-monetized; the most likely Content ID claim source); third-party/stock footage redistribution rights; speaker and minors' releases (both Sphere docs feature students); festival exclusivity windows (Fulfilling the Promise is on the 2026 circuit *now*); vendor work-for-hire terms (NODEHAUS / Kristi Kendall & Co hold or held masters for Faces, both 2025 Friedman films, Sphere econ series); grant/donor usage conditions; a one-page internal rights memo per republished video as the evidence file.

## 7 · Missing-Content Diagnosis

What the library **cannot** supply — the eventual new-production agenda, in priority order:

1. **A recurring host-led show.** Nothing in any library resembles the personality engine every studied competitor runs (Good Work's Toomey, Reason's weekly shows). The Faces/Paper Tiger formats prove the *film* capability; no property has the *host* capability. This is the single biggest structural gap.
2. **News-reactive quick-takes.** Zero capacity anywhere for 48–72h scholar reactions; the one template (Trump Accounts Short) is an isolated artifact, not a pipeline.
3. **Domestic-policy explainers.** The produced library is globalization/trade/China-heavy. Housing exists only as an interview; healthcare, immigration, and spending have **no produced video at all** — immigration especially, given it's a top Cato research area and a top news category. The 2014 Downsize series is the right concept with 12-year-old data.
4. **Criminal justice, visually treated** — pending inspection of the Neily series; if it's bare talking-head, this vein is empty despite being Cato's most cross-partisan material.
5. **Shorts-native production.** Everything current is cut-down clips; nothing is written for vertical.
6. **The fun register.** Nothing in any library qualifies as an experiment, list, myth-buster (one exception: the Declaration video), or surprising-fact piece — the whole seventh format tier is missing.
7. **Volume.** ~20 assets ≈ 12–16 weeks. Production planning must restart by week 8 or the channel goes quiet again in Q4.
