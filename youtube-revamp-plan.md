## Executive Summary

**The diagnosis:** Cato's channel (@catoinstitutevideo — 106K subs, 14.7M lifetime views, 2,302 videos) is an institutional archive with a dead pulse. It had **zero net subscriber growth in the last 30 days** despite 15 uploads; the last 3.7 years of output (+635 videos) bought only +19.7K subs. Roughly 12% of all lifetime views come from just 3 videos. The production system is optimized for record-keeping — every event and podcast, batch-uploaded under its internal event title — not for any viewer.

**The good news:** Cato's own catalog already proves what works. Every one of its rare hits — James Duane's "You Have the Right to Remain Innocent" (835.7K), "Introduction to Constitutional Law: 100 Supreme Court Cases" (526K), the Musk conversation (333K), the Sweden/COVID explainer (192K) — follows the same formula the five benchmark channels use deliberately: search/utility or question titles, a single charismatic person, a packaged sub-35-minute format, a name or news hook. Cato has hits; it has never treated them as R&D.

**The single most important proof point** comes from Reason TV, the closest peer: its two biggest videos ever — John Cleese (2.4M views) and Matt Taibbi (1.6M) — are *conference event footage*, the exact asset Cato produces weekly. The difference is packaging: single guest, scripted 60–90s cold open, conflict-framed title, chapters. Cato posted a sitting president (Milei) from its own conference nine weeks late, Spanish-audio-only, under a conference-program title: 1.3K views. Musk, from the *same conference, same upload day*, packaged with a name-first title: 333K. That 250x gap is the whole revamp in one anecdote.

**The plan in one paragraph:** Reposition the channel around a viewer promise ("what government policy actually does — with evidence, in plain English"), build 6 repeatable formats copied from proven competitor models (an event-to-interview pipeline, a question-led flagship explainer, a data-debunk essay, a Know-Your-Rights franchise, weekly scholar quick-takes, an original-Shorts doctrine), enforce a packaging playbook (no video ever ships under its event title), fix broken channel hygiene (orphan channels, wrong categories, batch-dumping, deletions), and run every proposed video through the weighted rubric in Phase 4.

**Top 5 recommendations by impact** (full ranked list at the end):
1. Stop posting raw event recordings — install the event-to-interview packaging pipeline (Reason: Cleese 2.4M / Taibbi 1.6M vs. Cato's Trump v. Barbara forum at 564 views).
2. Retitle everything for YouTube — no event/paper titles ever (Reason's verified practice; Milei 250x case study).
3. Mine the proven veins with sequels/franchises: Know Your Rights, law-study utility, name-get interviews (Cato's own top-3 evidence).
4. Launch one question-led flagship explainer/month with a host persona (Good Work: 3-person team, ~13.7M views/month; "Why can politicians trade stocks?" 1.2M).
5. Fix channel hygiene: orphan channels, categories, metadata, upload rhythm (zero-cost, currently self-defeating).

# PHASE 2 — Competitive Teardown

## 2.1 Reason TV — primary benchmark ([@ReasonTV](https://www.youtube.com/@ReasonTV))

**Scale (verified, Social Blade Jul 2026):** 1.39M subs · 537.8M views · 4,787 videos. Grew **+~500K subs / +240M views in ~2.5 years**, coinciding with output doubling to ~39 videos/month (Gillespie, Nov 2023).

**Formats:** "Great Moments in Unintended Consequences" comedy franchise (22+ volumes, ~3 min, rigid formula, reliably 300–600K views — [Vol. 13: 568K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5BDJIrvAQo)); Remy song parodies ([Thriller parody 732K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ynO-bqU6tUk)); documentaries ([Próspera 253K, 26:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TwiE1dxGYNY)); 8–10 min contrarian video essays ([Great Reset 1.5M at 8:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cd0OeC6Syd4)); data debunks ("Robert Reich's CEO Pay Chart Is Wrong. Here's the Real Math."); **event-harvested single-guest interviews — the channel's biggest hits ever: [Cleese 2.4M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mBf6kJIbXLg), [Taibbi 1.6M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kfyw3xA2hv0), both FreedomFest footage** with scripted 60–90s narrated cold opens and chapters; weekly personality shows (Just Asking Questions — Thursdays 1pm ET; Free Media) as the cheap volume layer.

**Length:** barbell — 1:40–3:10 comedy and 35–90 min interviews/podcasts; almost nothing in the dead middle except documentaries that earn it. **Titles:** claim+payoff two-sentence; name+conflict; genuine questions; **YouTube titles differ from magazine headlines by policy** (verified: Próspera doc retitled for YouTube). **Production tier:** deliberately mid — "a super-expensive TV commercial [look] with a green screen and a few thousand dollars" (Gillespie). **Cadence:** ~5 long-form/week + named-commentator Shorts bench.

**Steal:** (1) rigid-formula policy-failure franchise; (2) event→packaged-interview pipeline, never raw panels; (3) 8–10 min data-debunk essay; (4) title-for-YouTube discipline; (5) recurring named humans on fixed weekly slots, two-speed pipeline. **Don't copy:** out-joking Reason — Cato's whitespace is Reason's formats with deeper data rigor.

Phones are designed to be addicting.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NUMa0QkPzns)" 3.2M), grievance titles delivering systemic mechanisms ("[Why we all need subtitles now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYJtb2YXae8)" 17.2M). Motion graphics: maps as the workhorse ("[Israel-Palestine: a brief, simple history](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iRYZjOuUnlU)" 27.5M); 12fps graphic stutter; animation synced to narration. Two-tier cadence: 1–3 min news-pegged reporter takes (~daily) + roughly weekly/monthly crafted evergreen flagships.

**Steal:** (1) open with the number, not the institution; (2) package policy as the answer to a felt consumer problem; (3) one map-driven series with a fixed visual spine; (4) named scholar + fixed format + season structure (Cato's scholars are more institution-aligned than Vox's producers were); (5) two-tier cadence, bias flagships evergreen. **Don't copy:** 26–35 min docs/podcasts without an established star (their weakest current ROI).

## 2.5 Good Work — making dry topics watchable ([@GoodWorkMB](https://www.youtube.com/@GoodWorkMB))

**Identity verified:** Good Work, hosted/created by **Dan Toomey**, produced by Morning Brew. **Scale (verified):** 1.63M subs · 361.4M views · **only 390 videos** · ~13.7M views/month — with a core team of ~3. The efficiency benchmark of this study.

**Format:** ONE flagship 11–20 min "investigation" per ~3–4 weeks (comedic cold open → thesis question → chaptered explainer → 3–6 senior expert interviews → sign-off) + 2–4 **original** Shorts/week (sketches, news-reactive clips that link back to old longform via Related Shorts). **Titles are dinner-table questions** ("[So does private equity own everything?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M_leNmT7xvM)" 3.0M; "[Why can politicians trade stocks?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vT-u-SPj4_c)" 1.2M — *congressional-ethics policy outperforming nearly everything think tanks publish*). **Process (Toomey, on record):** research and expert interviews produce a completely straight script first; comedy is a punch-up pass at the end — substance is never traded away. They platform the opposing side on camera (the PE industry's own lobbyist). Production is deliberately "premium scrappy" ("if it feels too glossy, your mind glazes over"). Every thumbnail A/B/C tested.

**Steal:** (1) dinner-table-question packaging where question = title = thumbnail = cold open = structure; (2) straight-script-first, personality-second pipeline; (3) host as faux-naive interrogator — scholars are the straight men, host is the audience surrogate (do NOT make scholars do comedy); (4) original-Shorts doctrine funneling to evergreen longform; (5) monthly ambitious flagship > weekly mediocrity (watch-hours +212% in 2024 publishing every 3 weeks).

# PHASE 3 — The Revamp Plan

## 3.1 Positioning

**Channel promise (one sentence):** *"We read the bill, ran the numbers, and talked to the people it affects — here's what government policy actually does."*

**Target viewer:** the curious 25–45 professional who watches Vox, Good Work, and Johnny Harris — skeptical of both parties, allergic to cable-news outrage, willing to watch 12 minutes about zoning if it's packaged as a question they'd actually ask. Not the existing donor/scholar audience (they're already served by cato.org and events).

## 3.2 Content pillars and format menu

**Pillars (4):** ① Policy, Explained (evidence-led explainers) · ② Your Rights & the Constitution (the proven Duane/100-Cases vein) · ③ Unintended Consequences (policy-failure storytelling and data debunks) · ④ Big Conversations (name-get interviews harvested from events).

**Format menu — the repeatable shows:**

| # | Show (working name) | Length | Structure | Cadence | Production | Modeled on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The Cato Interview** (event-to-interview pipeline) | 30–50 min + 3–5 clips of 7–12 min each | Single guest only; scripted 60–90s narrated cold open establishing stakes; conflict/name-first title; full chapters; quote-first clips | Every event/conference → 1 interview + clips within 2 weeks | Editor + writer, ~1 day/video | Reason (Cleese 2.4M, Taibbi 1.6M); DSA clip pipeline (clips outperform parents) |
| F2 | **The flagship question explainer** | 10–15 min | Dinner-table question = title = thumbnail = cold open = structure; straight script by scholars first, host punch-up second; 3+ interviews incl. the opposing side; chaptered | 1/month | Host + producer + editor; 3–4 wk cycle | Good Work ("Why can politicians trade stocks?" 1.2M); Vox cold-open craft ("435." → 671.8K) |

**Event-recording conversion pipeline (F1, spelled out):** For every event: (1) identify the single strongest guest/segment — never publish the panel as-is; (2) cut to that guest; (3) script a 60–90s narrated intro establishing stakes and credibility; (4) retitle per the playbook; (5) add chapters; (6) cut 3–5 quote-titled 7–12 min clips, each opening on its quote at second zero; (7) cut 2–3 vertical Shorts; (8) schedule across days — never batch-dump; (9) full recording can still go to a "Full Events" playlist/podcast feed for the record, unlisted from the homepage. Foreign-language moments (the Milei lesson): subtitle/dub before publishing, ship within the news window.

## 3.3 Packaging playbook

**Title rules — no video ever ships under its event, paper, or program title.** Every title must be one of:
1. **Dinner-table question** — "Why is it illegal to build apartments?" / "Wait — who actually pays tariffs?" (Good Work model)
2. **Claim + kicker** (two sentences) — "The government loses money on student loans. On purpose." (DSA model)
3. **Name + conflict** — "Milei to Washington: You're Doing It All Wrong" (Reason model)
4. **Utility/search** — "What to Do When Police Stop You: A Lawyer Explains" (Cato's own Duane vein)
5. **Grievance → mechanism** — "Why your rent is too high" [delivers zoning] (Vox model)

Sentence case. No "#shorts" suffixes, no "A Cato expert explains," no colon-chained subtitles. First 15 seconds must pay off the title (quote-first for clips, bare-fact or anomaly open for explainers, urgency stamp for quick-takes).

**Thumbnail system** (verify current state by eye first — image inspection was blocked): one typeface family + one accent color pair, codified; a human face (scholar/guest close-up) on interviews and quick-takes; ≤4 words of text; one layout grammar per series so each show is recognizable at feed level (Vox/Verge model); **A/B/C test every thumbnail** (Good Work's verified practice). Chapters on everything ≥8 min (Verge: 10–17 per video).

**Naming conventions:** series get short names + numbered volumes ("The Receipts #12") — franchise branding compounds (GMUC is 22 volumes deep).

## 3.4 Channel architecture

1. **Trailer:** replace the mission-statement trailer with a 60–90s best-of sizzle cutting the channel's actual hits and the new shows' promises.
2. **Homepage shelves by viewer intent, not org chart:** Start Here (top hits) → Know Your Rights → Policy, Explained → The Receipts → Big Conversations → Shorts. Podcast/program playlists remain but move below the fold.
3. **Playlists:** consolidate to ~8–10 viewer-intent playlists; program-of-record playlists (Daily Podcast, event archive) stay for subscribers but aren't the front door.
4. **Identity cleanup:** delete or redirect the orphan "catovideo1" channel; fix cato.org links to the current handle; decide whether to pursue the @catoinstitute handle; correct every video's category to News & Politics; real tags.
5. **Channel description:** rewrite as the viewer promise + schedule, not the corporate mission.

## 3.5 Cadence and production plan

**Weekly rhythm (realistic for a small team, ~2 anchor shoots/week like DSA):**
- 1 packaged event interview or its clips (F1) — Tuesdays
- 1–3 scholar quick-takes (F5) — as news breaks
- 2–4 original Shorts (F6)
- Monthly: 1 flagship question explainer (F2) + 1–2 Receipts (F3)
- Quarterly: 1 Know Your Rights flagship (F4)

**Rules:** one upload slot per day maximum (kill batch-dumps); stop deleting videos (archive/unlist instead); every video gets a cato.org sources page linked in the description (the think-tank trust advantage no entertainer can match); every description carries chapters + one ritual CTA to a single owned destination (newsletter).

**Team implication `[ASSUMPTION]`:** this plan needs roughly a host/writer, a producer, and 1–2 editors — the Good Work benchmark is ~3 people producing 13.7M views/month; DSA ships 36 uploads/month from 2 weekly recordings. This is a workflow revamp more than a budget increase. Aim for "premium scrappy," not broadcast gloss — verified Toomey warning: "if it feels too glossy, your mind glazes over."

# PHASE 4 — Video Selection Rubric (weekly triage scorecard)

Score every proposed OR existing video 1–5 on each criterion. Multiply by weight; sum for a 1–5 weighted total.

| # | Criterion (weight) | What it measures | 1 (anchor) | 3 (anchor) | 5 (anchor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audience demand / dinner-table test (15%)** | Would a non-wonk lean in if this came up at dinner? Is there search/browse demand? | Internal milestone or program housekeeping ("Center 20th Anniversary") | Solid policy topic with a findable audience (Jones Act reform) | A question people already argue about or search for ("Why can politicians trade stocks?", "What to do when police stop you") |
| 2 | **Hook strength (15%)** | Can title + thumbnail + first 15s make ONE promise that the video pays off? | Only an event/paper title exists; opening is "Welcome to today's forum…" | A workable question or claim title, but the cold open needs manufacturing | The hook writes itself: a bare number, a quote, an anomaly ("435." / "just walked out of the meeting") |
| 3 | **Strategic / mission fit (12%)** | Does it advance Cato's core issues and authority, and would a new viewer understand what channel they've landed on? | Off-mission or purely reactive rage-bait | Mission-adjacent; fine but interchangeable with any outlet | Squarely on a pillar; only Cato could make it this credibly |
| 4 | **Format–audience fit (12%)** | Does it map to a format in the menu (F1–F6) with a proven comp? | Raw panel/webinar with no packaging plan | Fits a format with modifications | Drops cleanly into a menu format whose comp has verified demand |
| 5 | **Differentiation / authority edge (10%)** | Does Cato bring data, scholars, access, or rigor others can't? | Commodity take a pundit could tweet | Good angle, moderate edge | Primary-source math, unique scholar, or exclusive access (a Reich-debunk with BLS data; a head-of-state guest) |
| 6 | **Packaging potential (10%)** | Faces, visuals, story, chapters — can this LOOK like something? | Podium wide-shot, slides, no B-roll possible | Talking head + decent archival/graphics | Natural faces/field footage/map spine; thumbnail is obvious |
| 7 | **Production feasibility vs. return (10%)** | Cost/time against realistic view expectation for its format | Weeks of work for a format that historically does <1K | Standard effort, standard return | Cheap relative to its comp's proven ceiling (event footage already exists; quick-take shot in hours) |
| 8 | **Evergreen vs. timely value (8%)** | Will it earn search views in 2 years, OR is it inside a hot news window NOW? | Neither: stale news or dated framing | Moderately durable or moderately timely | Evergreen search magnet (rights, "who pays tariffs") or same-week news peg with a fast path to publish |
| 9 | **Clip / distribution potential (8%)** | Does it yield 3+ quote-first clips, Shorts, and a sources page? | Monolithic; nothing quotable | 1–2 usable moments | Clip machine: quotable guest, listable segments, Shorts-able stakes (DSA model: clips out-earn parents) |

**Weights sum to 100%.** Scoring guide: two scorers should land within 1 point on each criterion using the anchors; if they don't, the video's concept is underdefined — send it back.

**Thresholds (weighted total):**
- **≥ 4.0 — GREENLIGHT.** Schedule it; assign format, title, and thumbnail concept at greenlight time, not after the edit.
- **3.0–3.9 — REWORK.** Identify the two lowest-scoring criteria and fix them (usually #1 demand reframing or #2 hook) before re-scoring next week.
- **< 3.0 — KILL** (or divert to the archive/podcast feed without homepage placement).

**Auto-reject red flags (any one = kill regardless of score):**
- Raw multi-speaker panel/webinar with no single-guest cut planned
- Ships under its event/paper/program title
- Internal/anniversary/self-congratulation content
- No identifiable viewer question it answers
- Credibility risk: overclaims the data, misstates the study, or adopts outrage framing a scholar wouldn't sign (the engagement-vs-credibility tension resolves in credibility's favor — it's the moat)
- News peg already dead (>1 week old for commentary formats)

**Greenlight-regardless list (any one = make it, score later):**
- Head of state, Supreme Court advocate, or A-list name on Cato's stage (but F1 packaging is mandatory — the Milei rule)
- Same-day scholar reaction to a SCOTUS decision or major policy action squarely in Cato's lane
- Direct response to a viral claim (>1M views) that names Cato or misstates Cato research
- A proven-vein sequel (Know Your Rights, 100 Cases follow-up) — demand is already demonstrated

**Weekly triage ritual:** every pitch enters as one sentence — *the title it would ship under*. If no one can write the title, it fails criterion #2 by definition. Score, sort, greenlight the top of the stack against that week's production capacity.

# Recommendations Ranked by Impact

1. **Install the event-to-interview pipeline (F1); never post raw panels again.** Highest ceiling, lowest cost — the asset already exists. Evidence: Cleese 2.4M / Taibbi 1.6M vs. Cato's 564-view SCOTUS forum; the 250x Milei/Musk gap.
2. **Enforce the title rule channel-wide.** Zero cost. Evidence: Reason's verified retitling policy; every Cato hit already follows it.
3. **Mine the proven veins as franchises (F4 + sequels).** Demand demonstrated by Cato's own top videos (835.7K / 526K) and never followed up.
4. **Launch the monthly flagship question explainer (F2) with a host persona.** The compounding subscriber engine. Evidence: Good Work's 1.2M on congressional-ethics policy with a 3-person team.
5. **Launch The Receipts (F3).** Converts the existing op-ed pipeline into video; imports opposing audiences. Evidence: Reason's 1.5M essay; 8–10 min sweet spot.
6. **Fix channel hygiene (identity, categories, trailer, upload rhythm, no deletions).** Zero-cost drag removal (problems P2/P3/P5).
7. **Stand up quick-takes (F5) within news windows.** Recovers the value currently harvested by Reuters/Yahoo/C-SPAN (P6).
8. **Adopt the original-Shorts doctrine (F6)** — stop posting podcast fragments as Shorts. Evidence: Good Work 859K original Short vs. Cato's 700–2K clip fragments.
9. **Codify the thumbnail/design system and A/B/C test.** Compounds every other item; verify current thumbnails by eye first.
10. **Run the Phase 4 rubric as the standing weekly greenlight process** — the mechanism that keeps 1–9 from regressing to the event calendar.
