Executive Summary
Channel status: stalled

106,000 subscribers. Zero net growth last month.

Cato's channel is an institutional archive with a dead pulse — a production system built to file every event and podcast under its own program name, for an audience of nobody in particular. The catalog already contains the fix: every one of its four accidental hits follows the exact packaging formula that Reason TV, Vox, Dad Saves America, The Verge, and Good Work use on purpose. This document diagnoses the gap and specifies the repeatable formats, packaging rules, and weekly triage rubric to close it.

Seven problems beyond the obvious
P1

Growth is already dead

Zero net subscribers in 30 days despite steady uploads — a conversion failure, not a volume one.

P2

Fragmented identity

An abandoned duplicate channel and legacy URLs intercept brand searches before viewers reach the real one.

P3

Broken metadata

A SCOTUS forum filed under "Entertainment"; threadbare tags; foreign-language keynotes with no subtitles.

P4

Hits never get sequels

Four breakout videos, zero franchises built on any of them. Hits are treated as anomalies, not R&D.

P5

Batch-dump uploading

Multiple videos posted the same day split the impression budget; periodic deletions erase watch-history equity.

P6

News windows forfeited

Milei's keynote shipped 9 weeks late; Reuters and Yahoo Finance out-viewed Cato's own upload of its own event.

P7

Community signal is dead

29 likes / 4 comments on average — despite proof this audience engages heavily when given the chance.

Opens on a bare fact, not the institution: "435. That's how many people are in the House of Representatives…" — an apportionment-reform explainer at 671.8K views, the most Cato-adjacent hit in this study.

The Verge

  • The scholar who did the work presents it — packaged, not from the podium
  • Access/urgency cold opens: "I've only got 24 hours with this…"
  • One codified thumbnail/type/color system across every video

  • Question = title = thumbnail = cold open = structure, always
  • Straight script first (scholars), comedy punch-up second (host) — substance never traded away
  • Host plays faux-naive interrogator; scholars stay the straight man, never the comedian

PHASE 3

The Revamp Plan

"We read the bill, ran the numbers, and talked to the people it affects — here's what government policy actually does."

CHANNEL PROMISE · target viewer: a curious 25–45 professional who watches Vox and Good Work, skeptical of both parties, willing to watch 12 minutes on zoning if it's packaged as a question they'd actually ask
Format menu — six repeatable shows
F1

The Cato Interview

30–50 min + 3–5 clips · every event, within 2 weeks

Single guest only, never the raw panel. Scripted 60–90s cold open, conflict/name-first title, full chapters.

Modeled on Reason TV — Cleese 2.4M, Taibbi 1.6M
F2

The flagship question explainer

10–15 min · monthly

Dinner-table question = title = thumbnail = cold open = structure. Straight script by scholars first, host punch-up second.

Modeled on Good Work — 1.2M on congressional ethics
F3

The Receipts

8–10 min · 1–2/month, pegged to viral claims

Open on the viral claim in its own author's words, rebut with primary-source data, publish the sources page.

Modeled on Reason TV — Great Reset essay, 1.5M
F4

Know Your Rights

15–35 min + Shorts · quarterly flagship

One right or scenario per episode — police stops, IRS audits, property seizures. Single charismatic presenter, utility titles.

Modeled on Cato's own Duane video — 835.7K, never sequeled
F5

Scholar quick-takes

90 sec – 3 min · 1–3/week as news allows

The scholar who wrote the study reacts within 48–72 hours. Access/urgency cold open, links the standing explainer.

Modeled on The Verge — 1.7M on urgency openings
F6

Original Shorts doctrine

30–90 sec · 2–4/week

Written for vertical — one stake, not a podcast fragment. News-reactive Shorts link back to evergreen longform.

Modeled on Good Work — 859K on an original Short

PHASE 4

Video Selection Rubric

Score every proposed or existing video 1–5 on each criterion below; multiply by weight and sum for a weighted total. Two scorers should land within one point per criterion using these anchors — if they don't, the concept is underdefined.

Audience demand / dinner-table test

Would a non-wonk lean in at dinner? Is there findable search or browse demand?
15% weight
1Internal milestone or program housekeeping ("Center 20th Anniversary")
3Solid policy topic with a findable audience
5A question people already argue about or search for

Hook strength

Can title + thumbnail + first 15s make one promise the video pays off?
15% weight
1Only an event/paper title exists; opens with "Welcome to today's forum…"
3A workable question or claim title, cold open needs manufacturing
5The hook writes itself — a bare number, a quote, an anomaly

Strategic / mission fit

Advances Cato's core issues; a new viewer knows what channel they're on
12% weight
1Off-mission or purely reactive rage-bait
3Mission-adjacent but interchangeable with any outlet
5Squarely on a pillar; only Cato could make it this credibly

Format–audience fit

Maps to a menu format (F1–F6) with a proven comp
12% weight
1Raw panel/webinar with no packaging plan
3Fits a format with modifications
5Drops cleanly into a format whose comp has verified demand

Differentiation / authority edge

Data, scholars, access, or rigor others can't bring
10% weight
1Commodity take a pundit could tweet
3Good angle, moderate edge
5Primary-source math, a unique scholar, or exclusive access

Packaging potential

Faces, visuals, story, chapters — can this look like something?
10% weight
1Podium wide-shot, slides, no B-roll possible
3Talking head plus decent archival or graphics
5Natural faces, field footage, or a map spine — the thumbnail is obvious

Production feasibility vs. return

Cost/time against realistic view expectation for its format
10% weight
1Weeks of work for a format that historically does under 1K
3Standard effort, standard return
5Cheap relative to its comp's proven ceiling

Evergreen vs. timely value

Search-durable in 2 years, OR inside a hot news window right now
8% weight
1Neither — stale news or dated framing
3Moderately durable or moderately timely
5Evergreen search magnet, or a same-week news peg with a fast path to publish

Clip / distribution potential

Yields 3+ quote-first clips, Shorts, and a sources page
8% weight
1Monolithic; nothing quotable
3One or two usable moments
5A clip machine — quotable guest, listable segments, Shorts-able stakes
Thresholds — weighted total, 1 to 5 scale
KILL < 3.0
REWORK 3.0–3.9
GREENLIGHT ≥ 4.0
1.02.03.04.05.0

Auto-reject red flags — any one kills the score

  • Raw multi-speaker panel with no single-guest cut planned
  • Ships under its event, paper, or program title
  • Internal, anniversary, or self-congratulation content
  • No identifiable viewer question it answers
  • Credibility risk — overclaims data or adopts framing a scholar wouldn't sign
  • News peg already dead (>1 week old for commentary formats)

Greenlight-regardless — make it, score later

  • Head of state, SCOTUS advocate, or A-list name on Cato's stage (F1 packaging is mandatory — the Milei rule)
  • Same-day scholar reaction to a SCOTUS decision squarely in Cato's lane
  • Direct response to a viral claim (>1M views) that misstates Cato research
  • A proven-vein sequel — demand is already demonstrated
