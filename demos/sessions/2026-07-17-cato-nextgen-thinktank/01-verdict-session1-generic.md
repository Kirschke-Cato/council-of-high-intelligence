### The Thesis — What the Next-Generation Think Tank Is
The next-generation think tank is not a publisher of reports but a **staked-claims verification exchange**: a public ledger where every policy position is filed as a dated, falsifiable prediction with pre-registered resolution criteria, backed by the named author's own capital or clawback-able income. **Reality — not peer review or donor approval — resolves each claim**; funding routes retrospectively to demonstrated calibration rather than to credentials or narrative alignment; distribution is the leaderboard itself; and "staff" is anyone with a resolved track record, not a salaried fellow. The disrupted fundamentals are **verification and accountability** — and, one layer down, the institution's *maximand*: the incumbent externalizes the single thing that defines the category (what happened after the idea was enacted), and this model internalizes it as the product. The report was packaging; the claim-under-a-resolution-date is the essence. At the iPhone bar, this changes all six fundamentals at once, because production, funding, verification, distribution, staffing, and accountability collapse into one contract type: claim → stake → resolution → track-record.

### How the Fundamentals Change (Old Model → New Model)
- **Production:** 40-page unfalsifiable prose report → dated, numeric, falsifiable claim with pre-registered resolution criteria (LLMs a commodity drafting layer, not the moat).
- **Funding / business model:** prospective donor grants that buy conclusions → retrospective, outcome-contingent capital from buyers who *bear downside* (allocators, insurers, operators), routed to resolved track record.
- **Verification / credibility:** peer review by donor-aligned insiders → automatic resolution against public data via a persistent, contestable, decentralized adjudication function.
- **Distribution / influence:** PDF + masthead + media placement → queryable API / public leaderboard; the calibration score *is* the credibility signal.
- **Talent / staffing:** tenured, credentialed generalists → anyone with a portable Brier/consequence-weighted track record; entry by stake, not CV.
- **Accountability:** institution-as-shield, no one bears cost for being wrong → individual-as-stakeholder; being wrong publicly costs money and standing, by name.

### Acceptable Compromises
- **Coverage shrinks to the scoreable.** The model disciplines the ~30–90% of claims that decompose into clean predicates and *cedes* long-horizon, reflexive, and normative "what should we do" questions — arguably the most important ones — to a residue no ledger captures.
- **Neutrality perception and prestige.** It abandons the credential/masthead trust signal and the appearance of Olympian neutrality; trust must be re-earned continuously through resolved bets, which is slower to bootstrap.
- **Total addressable market narrows deliberately.** It gives up the entire ammunition-buying donor segment (most of today's funding base) to court the marginal outcome-bearing buyer.
- **Speed of capital.** Retrospective, resolution-gated payout is slower than instant-gratification patronage; some capital will not wait.

### Kill Criteria
- If a **willingness-to-pay test across ≥10 serious pitches to outcome-bearing buyers (allocators, insurers, operators) yields zero paying commitments within 6 months**, the demand-side thesis is falsified → the ledger is a rigorous artifact nobody funds; pivot or stop.
- If **retro-scoring ≥200 extracted predictions from incumbent flagship reports shows their accuracy is materially better than random within 3 months**, the "no error-correction loop" premise is weaker than claimed → the disruption is incremental, not iPhone-level.
- If, on a built pilot, **fewer than ~20% of consequential policy claims can be given clean pre-registered resolution criteria that survive adversarial contest within 12 months**, the mechanizable core is too small → thesis reduces to a niche forecasting tool.
- If **an incumbent (or AI-native rival) launches a genuinely staked, contestable scoreboard AND funders begin pricing analysts on it within 18 months**, the first-mover window is closed → the wedge is gone.

### Concrete Next Step
**Run a willingness-to-pay test with three outcome-bearing buyers (one allocator, one insurer, one operator) by mid-September 2026 (≈8 weeks)** — pitch "buy a calibrated track record, not conclusions" and measure real commitment before architecting any resolution machinery.

### Unresolved Questions
- **Does the downside-bearing buyer actually exist and pay?** The entire model rests on a funder class that wants calibration over cover. This is asserted, not demonstrated — and is the single binding constraint (Kahneman, Socrates, Alan Watts, Munger).
- **How do you resolve reflexive claims** whose publication changes the outcome they predict? Named unsolved by Ada and Ilya.
- **How do you resolve slow, confounded, multi-decade, causally-attributed policy claims** without the resolution step becoming theater or a gameable extraction problem (Karpathy: resolution is itself an ML-shaped classification task with error and adversarial surface)?
- **What scoring function is correct** — calibration/Brier (most members' default) or consequence-weighted payoff (Taleb, dealbreaker)? These give different institutions.
- **Who fronts analysts' capital during the multi-year resolution gap, and does that relocate patronage upstream into stake-selection** (Machiavelli)?
- **Can the resolution layer be governed against its own capture** — or does "whoever owns resolution owns the category" simply mint a new gatekeeper (Meadows, Lao Tzu)?
- **Is the near-unanimous convergence signal or availability cascade?** Tetlock/Metaculus is the most retrievable template; tight agreement on an unbuilt mechanism should lower, not raise, confidence (Kahneman).

### Recommended Next Steps
1. **Feynman's diagnostic (parallel, ≤3 months):** retro-score ≥200 implicit predictions from five-year-old incumbent flagship reports to empirically demonstrate the missing feedback loop — this is both proof and marketing.
2. **Draft the resolution-criteria protocol** as pre-committed, transparent, adversarially contestable rules *before* launch (Sun Tzu, Aristotle, Ada) — the incumbents' co-optation move is a cosmetic dashboard with soft rules.
3. **Specify governance of the resolver** with decentralization, rotating/independently-appointed adjudicators, and term limits (Meadows, Ilya) to design against capture from day one.
4. **Ship the boring core first** — claim → stake → scheduled resolution → track-record, on the cleanly-resolvable subset — and add adversarial-refutation / tiered-milestone / reflexivity machinery only when gaming appears in production logs (Linus, Lao Tzu, Karpathy).
5. **Decide the scoring function explicitly** and stress-test consequence-weighting vs. calibration against historical tail cases before locking it (Taleb).
6. **Build provenance disclosure into the consumption interface** — who staked, what they wanted, author's prior hit-rate, at the point of decision (Rams).

### Consensus & Agreement
**Consensus reached, and it is a composite.** All 18 members converged on one core mechanism — replace the report with a staked, dated, falsifiable claim; let reality resolve it; route funding and standing to resolved track record; make the ledger/leaderboard the distribution. No member proposed a competing thesis (nobody defended "faster report factory" or "AI-drafted prose" as sufficient — all called it "a rotary phone with a color screen").

The **composite institutional design** integrates the distinct, non-redundant contributions:
- **Core contract** (all): claim → stake → resolution → track-record funding → scoreboard distribution.
- **Demand-first sequencing** (Socrates, Kahneman, Watts, Munger, Machiavelli): build *for* the buyer who already bears outcome risk; run a WTP test before architecting.
- **Capture-resistant governance of the resolver** (Meadows, Machiavelli, Ada, Ilya, Sun Tzu): unowned/decentralized, contestable, pre-committed rules.
- **Consequence-weighted scoring, not Brier** (Taleb, dealbreaker).
- **Adversarial resolution oracle** as the real moat under AI-scale claim generation (Ilya, dealbreaker).
- **Retrospective funding** severing the prospective patronage circuit (Lao Tzu, Musashi, Taleb).
- **Provenance disclosure at consumption** (Rams).
- **Ship-boring-core-first discipline** (Linus, Lao Tzu, Feynman, Karpathy) and **timing/first-mover urgency** (Musashi, Sun Tzu).

### Key Insights by Member
- **Aristotle**: The category error is treating the report (packaging) as the product; the essence is "a claim under a resolution date." Also the sharpest scope limit — the model wins only in the funder-*species* whose telos is decision-quality, not all funders.
- **Socrates**: Inverted the question to "who is authorized to be believed, and what act confers it?" — and crystallized that the mechanism is trivial while the real disruption is manufacturing a downside-bearing buyer.
- **Sun Tzu**: Named verification-at-scale as the currently-unowned chokepoint, and the incumbent counter-move (cosmetic dashboards without stakes) to pre-empt with pre-committed, contestable resolution rules.
- **Ada Lovelace**: Reduced the whole thing to a five-transition state machine and drew the hard line — resolution is *not* a free transition; deleting the institution merely hides the irreducible judgment-bearing resolver.
- **Marcus Aurelius**: Accountability is the one fundamental fully within our control; put the downside on the *named individual*, not the brand — and reframed adoption as values-recruitment, "difficult is not impossible."
- **Machiavelli**: Revealed preference (budgets, not mission statements) shows the product is laundered conviction; warned that capital-fronting during the resolution gap relocates patronage upstream into stake-selection.
- **Lao Tzu**: The category should *dissolve*, not improve — a thin, unowned settlement layer where reality does the verifying; subtract the producing institution rather than build a better one.
- **Feynman**: The smallest checkable unit is a dated numeric claim, not a report; proposed the runnable proof — retro-score old flagship reports; and called out jargon that doesn't cash out to new behavior.
- **Linus Torvalds**: Treat claims like code with CI — ship the boring claim-ledger + scheduled resolution job, delete the fellows/PDF/mission apparatus, resist premature abstraction.
- **Miyamoto Musashi**: Timing is the neglected fundamental — the verification/distribution cost structure *just* broke; launch now with retrospective calibration-weighted funding before an incumbent co-opts the form.
- **Alan Watts**: Reframed the function as "converting funded conviction into influence"; the disruptable fundamental is who pays and what payment buys — court the buyer who already bears downside.
- **Andrej Karpathy**: "Scoring + feedback improves calibration" is the proven capability (not "AI writes better policy"); resolution itself is a gameable ML-shaped extraction problem, not a deterministic oracle.
- **Ilya Sutskever** *(dealbreaker)*: Under AI-scale claim generation, the *adversarial resolution oracle* — bounty-funded refutation, independent resolvers, tiered milestones — is the moat, not the pipe; convincingness decouples from truth.
- **Daniel Kahneman**: The disruptable fundamental is the *maximand*, not the format; the council's own unanimity is an availability cascade; run a cheap disconfirming WTP test before architecting.
- **Donella Meadows [1.5×]**: Loop A (donor-reward, fast) out-competes Loop B (outcome-feedback, 5–20yr delay); the fix is collapsing Loop B inside the funding cycle — and the registry must carry a balancing loop against its *own* capture.
- **Charlie Munger**: Inversion — the surest failure is AI-accelerating the report while leaving incentives intact; raised confidence only via cross-disciplinary triangulation, and flagged consequence-blind scoring's Goodhart trap.
- **Nassim Taleb** *(dealbreaker)*: Policy lives in Extremistan — score by *consequence-weighted payoff*, never Brier/calibration, which prices the catastrophic tail at zero; barbell 90% near-certain / 10% large-stake tail bets; skin in the game as the product.
- **Dieter Rams**: Separated the two conflated users (deadline-pressed staffer vs. cover-seeking funder); the non-negotiable minimum is provenance disclosure — who paid, what they wanted, prior hit-rate — at the point of consumption.

### Points of Disagreement
- **Owned vs. unowned resolution.** Lao Tzu insists on a thin, *unowned* settlement layer with no proprietor; Ada, Ilya, Machiavelli, and Meadows argue a persistent judgment-bearing resolver is irreducible and can only be governed/decentralized, not deleted — and that "no owner" just hides the capture surface.
- **Build-first vs. demand-first.** Musashi, Linus, and Marcus Aurelius say ship the core now (demand is secondary); Socrates, Kahneman, and Alan Watts say demand is the *binding* constraint — a ledger sold to today's donors dies, so identify the outcome-bearing buyer first.
- **Scoring function.** Most members default to calibration/Brier; Taleb (dealbreaker) holds that frequency-scoring is categorically wrong for a fat-tailed domain — a live, unreconciled design fork.
- **Complexity at launch.** Ilya (dealbreaker) makes the adversarial oracle the day-one centerpiece; Linus, Lao Tzu, and Feynman call that shipping unsolved research and would add it only when gaming appears in production.
- **How much survives the residue.** Aristotle, Lao Tzu, Feynman, and Karpathy concede that long-horizon, reflexive, and normative claims escape any ledger — several worry the model optimizes the trivial and starves the vital.

### Minority Report
No member dissented from the core thesis, but two logged **DEALBREAKER=yes** design constraints that the majority did not adopt as binding:
- **Ilya Sutskever — adversarial resolution is non-negotiable.** A naive ledger gets Goodharted and captured once AI generates and persuades at scale and once forecasters influence the outcomes they predict. If the exchange ships without a bounty-funded adversarial refutation market and independently-appointed resolvers, it optimizes convincingness over truth. Strongest argument: the resolution layer, not the ledger, is the actual moat — building the easy 20% and hand-waving the frontier 80% ships a capturable system.
- **Nassim Taleb — never score by calibration/Brier.** In Extremistan, a pristine calibration record can belong to the analyst who greenlit a catastrophic tail; frequency-weighted scoring prices the tail at zero and reproduces the exact fragility being disrupted. Strongest argument: the whole point is internalizing consequence — a scoreboard that ranks by hit-rate incentivizes farming cheap, low-consequence claims, which is the disease.
- **Standing dissenting caution (Kahneman, not a dealbreaker but the sharpest challenge that survived cross-examination):** the near-unanimity is itself suspect (availability cascade around Tetlock), and the reference class of rigor-first reforms — PolitiFact, replication initiatives, prediction markets — is near-total failure to displace incumbents because rigor was never the binding constraint. This is why the Concrete Next Step is a disconfirming demand test, not a build.
