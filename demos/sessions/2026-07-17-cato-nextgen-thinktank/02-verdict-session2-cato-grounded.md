### The Thesis — What the Next-Generation Think Tank Is
The fundamental Cato disrupts is **verification and accountability**: the industry's oldest unspoken privilege is that a think tank is *never measurably wrong* — publication counts as impact and no loop ever disconfirms a losing argument. The next-generation Cato is the first think tank to carry a **public, external, adversarially-resolved track record** — call it the **Cato Record** — built on the one asset no rival owns: a court docket. Every output decomposes into two layers: **axioms** (the moral case — liberty as the highest political value, coercion bears the burden of proof) that stay argued, unscored, and general-funded; and **empirical corollaries** ("this statute is unconstitutional on ground G"; "this regulation raises prices X% within Y years") that are tagged at publish time as dated, falsifiable claims and resolved by reality Cato does not control — court rulings and citations, CBO/GAO/BLS/Fed data, enacted legislation. There is **no market, no staking, no odds, no public leaderboard, and no calibration-tied funding** — those relocate the mission into a metric that Goodharts principle into propaganda. This is not a prediction market; it is a *receipts* institution where the moral argument stays sharp and the empirical scaffolding under it becomes honest, dated, and externally adjudicated — turning the Levy Center's amicus history from a pile of briefs into a purpose-built, unfakeable ledger of predictions reality already ruled on.

### How the Fundamentals Change (Old Model -> New Model)
- **Verification:** peer/editorial review and "we were principled" -> dated, falsifiable corollaries resolved by external forums (courts, government data, legislative votes) Cato cannot game.
- **Accountability:** publication = impact; no disconfirmation loop -> an embedded dated-claim/resolved-outcome ledger that closes the missing balancing loop and surfaces misses.
- **Production of ideas:** DC scholar authors a one-way study -> claims filed as litigation-shaped, falsifiable positions flowing research→brief→classroom as one current; (minority adjunct) Sphere's educators surface state-level cases as inputs.
- **Distribution/influence:** downloads and media hits as vanity signal -> a court-verified track record that is itself the credibility artifact, distributed through existing channels.
- **Funding/business model:** unchanged by design -> stays donor-blind, patient, individual-donor-led; verification is a *rules* change, never a *money* change (protects independence).
- **Talent/staffing:** reputation on volume and media hits -> reputation additionally on claims that survive external adjudication, with a small (~2-3 FTE) analyst function to code and resolve claims.

### Fit with Cato — Principles, Independence, and Assets
The model is engineered to *preserve* Cato's four principles and independence rather than trade them. The moral axiom is never scored — coercion's burden of proof stays an ethical claim, not a forecast — so the mission is not redefined as accuracy. Funding stays strictly donor-blind and patient: no calibration-tied money, nothing resembling "pay for a resolved bet," which would breach the no-donor-directed-research covenant. Nonpartisan posture is untouched; courts and CBO/GAO resolve claims issue-by-issue. **Leverages:** the Levy Center amicus program (the one engine where reality already adjudicates), existing publications, the Sphere/Cato University/Courses pipeline as distribution (and, per the minority, as production), the individual-donor base, and the just-banked $300M campaign slack that lets this be built as deliberate infrastructure, not a panic pivot. **Strands:** the standalone policy-study PDF as terminal artifact, pure media-hit metrics, and any calibration/prediction-market front-end. **On the prior thesis: ADAPTED, not adopted — and its core build REJECTED.** Zero members affirmed the staked-claims exchange as built. The council kept its one true diagnosis (Cato has no disconfirmation loop) and its "reality resolves claims" instinct, but rejected the market, the staking, the public leaderboard, and calibration-as-funding as mission-corrupting, organizationally unbuildable, and a category error (scoring a fat-tailed persuasion mission by average accuracy).

### Acceptable Compromises
- **Scope narrowing toward the justiciable.** Litigation and government data resolve only some claims; monetary and foreign policy have no docket. The ledger covers a subset, and over-reliance on it could quietly bias the institution toward court-resolvable questions. Named openly, not hidden.
- **Editorial friction and legal exposure.** Scholars must commit to falsifiable language before publication — slower first drafts, legal review for anything touching live litigation, and the reputational cost of a publicly surfaced miss.
- **No single flashy "iPhone moment" product.** The disruption is a discipline layered onto existing outputs, not a new consumer artifact; the novelty is institutional honesty, which is quieter than a gadget.
- **Delay.** Rights cases resolve on 2–4 year cycles, so the feedback loop is slower than the iPhone/Tesla analogy implies; the ledger is Lindy, not fast.

### Kill Criteria
- If, within 18 months of go-live, tracked corollary output shifts measurably toward safe/quickly-resolvable claims (a drop in novel or contrarian corollaries versus the retrospective baseline), the mission-narrowing failure is real -> fully decouple the ledger from any review or compensation signal and keep it purely descriptive.
- If Cato Partner giving or a donor-sentiment survey shows the surfaced misses being read as weakness (net-negative giving effect) within 18 months -> retreat the ledger to internal-only, drop the public-facing layer.
- If under ~30% of tagged corollaries actually resolve within their stated windows by 24 months (litigation too sparse/slow, data too ambiguous), the ledger covers too little to matter -> narrow to a litigation-only "brief-to-bill" record or abandon.
- If hostile press weaponizes early resolved misses into a durable "Cato was wrong N% of the time" narrative that measurably dents earned-media credibility within 12 months -> reframe or firewall the public presentation before expanding coverage.

### Concrete Next Step
Build a retrospective proof-of-concept ledger — tag and resolve the falsifiable corollaries in the last three years of Levy Center amicus briefs and major economic testimony against actual court rulings/citations and CBO/GAO/BLS outcomes — by the end of Q4 FY2026, owned by the Levy Center plus one research analyst.

### Unresolved Questions
- **Donor reaction is unknown.** No member had data on whether Cato Partners, who give on principle and conviction, reward visible honesty or read surfaced misses as weakness. This is the single biggest unmeasured variable and gates the public-vs-internal decision.
- **Resolution rate and ambiguity.** How often do litigation outcomes actually track the research claim, and how many "resolutions" require an adjudicator (recreating peer review under a new name)? Court outcomes turn on procedure and judge selection as much as truth.
- **Coverage of docket-less domains.** How does Cato hold monetary, foreign, and trade policy accountable when no court or clean data event resolves them, without stranding them?
- **Scholar buy-in.** Will senior scholars — who currently answer to no accountability mechanism — accept falsifiable pre-commitment, or lobby the board to kill visibility? Genuine intellectual pride may cut either way.
- **Whether the minority adjuncts are load-bearing.** Is the Sphere-as-producer inversion or a Constitutional Options Desk necessary to the disruption, or optional composables the core ledger does not require?

### Recommended Next Steps
1. Run a small donor-signal test: present the retrospective proof-of-concept (including honest misses) to a stewardship sample of Cato Partners and measure reaction before any public launch.
2. Define the claim-tagging schema and resolution protocol {claim, author, date, falsification criterion, resolution source, outcome} as machine-readable metadata embedded at publish time.
3. Pilot the Sphere-as-producer inversion narrowly: have a cohort of educators surface state-level liberty cases (occupational licensing, civil forfeiture, takings) and route the strongest falsifiable ones to Levy — test Watts's bet that educators produce, not just consume.
4. Stand up a lightweight "Constitutional Options Desk" experiment (Taleb): pre-stage litigation/legislation-ready arguments against 3–5 named overreach scenarios; measure whether pre-positioning shortens Cato's response time when a crisis lands.
5. Explicitly document the docket-less-domain gap and design a non-litigation resolution path (economic data, enacted legislation) so scope does not silently narrow.

### Consensus & Agreement
Consensus reached on the core. Fourteen-to-fifteen members converged on the **embedded litigation-anchored dated-claim ledger** with the axiom/corollary decomposition, external resolution, no market/leaderboard/calibration-funding, and donor-blind funding preserved. What survived deliberation across every lens: (1) reject the staked-claims *exchange* as built; (2) keep its diagnosis and "reality resolves claims" instinct; (3) never stake the moral axiom; (4) stake only falsifiable empirical corollaries; (5) resolve via assets Cato already owns/faces; (6) protect independence by keeping funding untouched. The synthesis is best read as a **composite**: a consensus core (the Cato Record ledger) plus two composable, non-consensus adjuncts raised by dealbreaker dissenters — Watts's Sphere-as-producer network and Taleb's Constitutional Options Desk barbell — either of which can bolt onto the core without contradicting it.

### Points of Disagreement
- **Public vs. internal ledger.** Sun Tzu and Musashi want it public and loud as a costly signal that claims the category before rivals; Rams, Karpathy, and Kahneman want resolution tracked internally to serve real users and avoid a scoreboard hostile press can weaponize. Unresolved, and gated by the unknown donor reaction.
- **How much to build.** Watts and Sutskever/Karpathy split on AI: Sutskever's instinct to scale formation through a model was withdrawn as unbuildable, and Watts rejects any formation engine outright — while the Lao Tzu/Feynman/Linus wing insists on building *nothing new* beyond the tag-and-log ledger.
- **Litigation as the anchor.** Kahneman flagged that fifteen members anchoring on the docket may be an availability cascade, not independent confirmation — courts resolve a thin, procedurally-contingent slice; Meadows independently warned this narrows scope. The council did not fully resolve how to hold docket-less domains accountable.
- **Reactive log vs. forward pre-positioning.** Taleb and Musashi want arguments *pre-staged before* events (options desk); the ledger majority logs claims *after* publication and resolves them later — composable, but a real difference in posture.

### Minority Report
- **Alan Watts (dealbreaker=yes) — Distributed liberty-formation network.** The core ledger treats Sphere as distribution; Watts argues the real disruption is inverting 50,000 educators into the *production and field-testing* layer, surfacing real state-level liberty cases that feed Levy — with the ledger merely the verification surface. His dealbreaker (reject any AI formation engine as indoctrination; keep the axiom unscored) is honored by the winning thesis. Strongest argument: the ledger alone changes verification but leaves *production* centralized and linear — the un-disrupted fundamental.
- **Nassim Taleb (dealbreaker=yes) — Constitutional Options Desk barbell.** Even a delay-tolerant ledger is backward-looking; Taleb argues policy influence is fat-tailed and the convex payoff comes from pre-staging litigation/legislation-ready arguments against named overreach scenarios so Cato fires first when a crisis hits. His dealbreaker (no calibration-as-funding) is honored. Strongest argument: a resolution-optimized ledger still risks starving the slow, unpopular, high-conviction bets that are Cato's reason to exist — the options desk protects exactly those.
- Note: both dissents are *additive* to the consensus, not opposed to it. Neither dealbreaker is violated by the winning thesis, so no dissent blocks the verdict; both are logged as recommended experiments.

### Epistemic Diversity Scorecard
- **Perspective spread (4/5):** genuinely orthogonal lenses (systems structure, power/incentive, ML training-signal, Stoic control, antifragility, user-centered design, categorization) — but they converged hard onto one mechanism, and Kahneman explicitly flagged the convergence as a possible availability cascade rather than fully independent confirmation.
- **Provider spread (1/5):** single provider (Claude-only, anthropic); Chairman shares the provider.
- **Evidence mix:** ~35% mechanistic, ~25% strategic, ~15% ethical, ~13% empirical, ~12% heuristic.
- **Convergence risk (High):** near-unanimity on the litigation-anchored ledger is striking, but it rests on a single-provider monoculture and, by Kahneman's own diagnosis, may reflect the vividness/availability of the courtroom as a resolver rather than its actual coverage. The two features that would most stress-test the consensus — donor reaction and docket-less-domain accountability — remain unmeasured, so the agreement is provisional pending the retrospective proof-of-concept and donor-signal test.
